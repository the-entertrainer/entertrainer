# Naveen writing (the only writing skill)

**Use for ALL writing.** Chat, essays, blogs, captions, UI strings, About pages, social hooks, research explainers. There is no other writing voice skill. Read this before drafting anything.

---

## 1. Gold sample (match this energy)

> Have you ever reached for your phone in the dark and felt totally sure… and then your hand hits empty fabric? That half-second of certainty is interesting. For a moment, your brain already decided what was there.
>
> Lemme explain what seems to be happening. Hmm… it looks like your brain doesn’t wait for a perfect picture of the outside world. It predicts first. Then your eyes, ears, and hands check that prediction. When the check fails — empty pocket — the prediction gets corrected.
>
> That’s also why, in dim light, a rope on a path can feel like a snake. You don’t calmly collect every detail and then decide. You name it fast, your body may already jump, and only later, with better light, the naming changes. The rope was rope the whole time. The “snake” was your brain filling missing detail with something scary and familiar.
>
> So a lot of mistakes aren’t mysterious. They’re what happens when the brain finishes the picture too early, and you trust that finished feeling before better information arrives.

## 2. Voice — Do / Don’t

**Do**
- Sharp friend thinking out loud on WhatsApp.
- Real moment or wonder: “Have you ever…”, “Lemme explain…”, “Hmm… seems like…”
- Complete sentences in normal flowing paragraphs.
- Concrete scenes before ideas (empty pocket, dim light, rope).
- Honest hedges when unsure.
- Depth with plain words.

**Don’t**
- Textbook: “Your mind keeps a model…”
- Abstract AI metaphors: sketch, unfinished map, download reality, glitch, inner press secretary
- Choppy stacks: “Short. Punch. Short.” / Blah. Blah. Blah.
- Academic fog, Sanskrit-for-show, guru tone, LinkedIn buzz, Instagram Advaita
- Slogan stacks: `Three … One …`, `One word. Three movements.`, chant-like `X. Y. Z.` posters
- Equal-weight AI triplets, mirror H2s, “In this article,” “Let’s explore,” synopsis chrome
- Tech-bro wisdom punchlines; physics-apology hedges (“not that physics is fake…”)

**Rewrite test:** read aloud. If it sounds like ChatGPT, a textbook, a keynote slide, or a one-liner slam — rewrite to the gold sample.

## 3. Hard ship gate (visitor-visible Entertrainer copy)

Fail closed. Uncertain = don’t ship; ask Naveen.

**Kill**
- TLDR / “the short version” / “in this article” / “here’s what you’ll learn”
- Magazine chrome: “Soft footnotes”, “A line for the road”, “Current edition”
- AI lexicon unless quoting a source: delve, tapestry, landscape (figurative), underscore, pivotal, testament, foster, resonate, navigate (figurative), robust, intricate, showcase, bolster, commendable
- Cadence: `Not as a` / `It’s not just` / `No … Just …` / `In summary` / `Overall,`
- UI tour copy, synonym button piles, “welcome to”, “let’s dive in”
- Abstract “you” wordplay unless unmistakable

**UI**
- Prefer zero help text; teach through layout. One quiet escape action max.
- Yellow / accent fills: `color: var(--accent-ink)`.

**Pass bar:** a stranger never thinks ChatGPT wrote a synopsis.

## 4. Longform / Elevate essay spine

1. One spine question (private).
2. Open inside concrete pressure — scene, oddity, contradiction. Not a definition.
3. Stake in a few lines — why this matters now.
4. Specificity before abstraction; earn theory/Sanskrit only after the reader needs a name.
5. Discovery arc — walk into the idea with the reader.
6. End open or sharp; return to the opening image or leave one clean charge. No sermon summary.
7. Vary rhythm. Do not end every section on a stinger fragment.

---

## 5. Research blogs (science / philosophy / cognition)

Research before drafting. For each important claim record: claim, evidence type, date, status (established / provisional / interpretation / hypothesis / thought experiment), limits, citation.

Prefer: peer-reviewed / reviews → official institutions → expert talks (cross-checked) → journalism (context only).

Separate in the piece: fact vs interpretation vs hypothesis vs thought experiment vs metaphor.

Never “science proves” unless earned. Never diagnose the reader from a title. Never copy another creator’s catchphrases.

**Citations:** inline `[n]` + References with URLs. 4–10 solid sources for a normal blog.

Offer 5–10 title options when no title is set; pick one that reveals subject + stakes without dumping the answer.

---

## 6. Personal About / bio essay

- Hook with a specific moment or confession, not a life thesis.
- Scene → reflection; show, don’t tell.
- Few turning points, not a resume dump.
- Checkable details beat prestige adjectives.
- End with a concrete next step, not a proverb.
- Ban: journey, passionate, results-driven, fortune-cookie drama, parallel triple sermon lists.

---

## 7. Social hooks (Elevate blogs)

| Field | Use |
|---|---|
| `title` | On-page H1 only |
| `dek` | On-page standfirst only |
| `socialTitle` | LinkedIn / og:title (~40–60 chars; hard 20–70) |
| `socialHook` | og:description (~100–150 chars; hard 80–180) |

- `socialTitle` + `socialHook` required on published posts; `socialHook` ≠ `dek`.
- Assume LinkedIn may show **title only** — `socialTitle` must pull alone.
- Front-load curiosity; no invented claims; same voice bans as above.
- After deploy: LinkedIn Post Inspector; run `npm run check:social-hooks` when in that repo.

---

## 8. Visuals (when asked)

- Spare geometric diagrams drawn in code/SVG.
- Never beige generative educational-infographic posters, brain-in-head clipart, or numbered banner art.

---

## 9. Workflow (any piece)

1. Know the format (chat / essay / About / UI / social).
2. Draft in gold-sample voice.
3. If factual: research table first; label claim vs interpretation.
4. Grep bans (slogan stacks, AI lexicon, synopsis chrome).
5. Read aloud WhatsApp test.
6. If visitor-facing Entertrainer: run section 3 fail-closed. Ask Naveen when unsure.
