# General-visitor voice audit

## Core judgement

The current site is **clearer than a portfolio for specialists**, but it still sounds as though it expects the visitor to understand learning-design practice before they arrive. Terms such as *learning practice*, *demonstration*, *case study*, *instructional design*, *feedback*, *storyboard*, and *assessment* may be familiar to a colleague but do not help a family member, recruiter from another field, client, or curious visitor immediately understand what Naveen makes.

The deeper problem is not only vocabulary. The first screen says what the professional discipline is, but says less about the person doing it: the hospitality background, the enjoyment of making difficult things feel straightforward, the habit of building the tool when a repetitive task becomes frustrating, and the wish to show the unfinished thinking rather than only the polished result. Those are the parts that make the site feel like Naveen rather than a generic design studio.

## Plain-language replacement decisions

| Current framing | General-visitor issue | Revised direction |
|---|---|---|
| “Learning design, demonstrated in the open” | Abstract and insider-facing | “I make complicated things easier to learn” |
| “Independent learning practice” | Sounds like a consultancy label | “Naveen Jose’s corner of the internet” |
| “Practice” | Ambiguous without context | “Try a lesson” |
| “Projects” and “case studies” | A little formal and portfolio-like | “Things I’ve made” and “The story behind the work” |
| “Tools” and “practical utilities” | Functional but impersonal | “Free little tools that save time” |
| “Instructional design” in the lead | A label before an explanation | “I help people understand how to do things” |
| “Work with its workings attached” | Clever but opaque | “A few things I’m proud to show you” |

## Voice rules

The revised voice will use short ordinary verbs—**make, show, explain, try, save, help, build**—before professional terms. It will describe a professional method only after the visitor has seen its human purpose. The homepage will use first person where that makes the maker visible, and it will keep one lightly playful line only when it is specific to Naveen’s own way of working.

The professional term **instructional design** remains useful on its dedicated page and in supporting metadata, but it will be paired with an explanation: “the work of making a complicated task easier for someone else to learn.” Similarly, individual tool pages can keep precise terms such as storyboard or assessment because visitors arrive there through a plain-language promise.

## Implemented clarity checks

The homepage now opens with a first-person introduction, a concrete hospitality origin, and the plain-language promise, “I make complicated things click.” Its main navigation reads **Try a lesson**, **Things I made**, **Free tools**, **About me**, and **How this site works**. The corresponding route introductions now explain the visitor benefit before using profession-specific detail.

At `375 × 812`, the opening experience is readable without prior context: the wordmark, theme and menu controls, personal introduction, one-sentence purpose, and the single action, **Try a four-minute lesson**, appear in a clear order without clipping or a dense professional label.
