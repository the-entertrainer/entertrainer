# The SEWA Chronicles: Anti-Generic Content Audit

## Reader need

>A portfolio reader needs to understand what Club Mahindra asked Naveen to make, what he personally did while working as a housekeeper, and what the finished comic contained, so that they can judge the work and the responsibility involved.

## Method

This audit used the `anti-generic-content` skill. Each visible line was tested for user need, factual value, duplication, ordinary-language plausibility, and whether it could be replaced by a more concrete detail. The standard is based on user needs, restraint, and concise objective writing rather than an invented “voice.” [1] [2] [3]

## Clean book structure

The revised book should be a normal project post distributed across publication pages, not a named storytelling device.

| Page group | Purpose | Plain content direction |
|---|---|---|
| Cover | Identify the project | Original SEWA cover only. |
| 1. Commission | Explain the starting point | Club Mahindra asked for a sixteen-page comic. Naveen was working as a housekeeper and early in his career. |
| 2. What the book needed to do | State the brief | Turn real service moments into short stories that staff could read and discuss. |
| 3. Finding stories | Show the reporting | Staff conversations and specific incidents, including the bracelet, the child at dinner, the rain, and the stranded guest. |
| 4. Making pages | Show the work | Notes, thumbnail, panels, lettering, colour, correction. |
| 5–9. Comic pages | Let the comic lead | Original page plus one direct note only when it identifies a real decision behind the adaptation. |
| 10. Finished book | State the result | Sixteen pages were completed, illustrated, and laid out for Club Mahindra Learning and Development. |
| 11. Credit | Attribute accurately | Naveen’s role, client, date, and original-art note. |

## Illustrative plain rewrite

This sample demonstrates the approved direction but does not change published copy.

> Club Mahindra asked me to make a sixteen-page comic from service stories at the resort. At the time, I was working as a housekeeper and was still early in my career.
>
> I started by asking people what had happened. A guest was stranded on the road. A bracelet was found after checkout. A child would not stop crying at dinner. Those moments became the pages.
>
> Each page began with notes. I wrote a short scene, planned the panels, drew the characters, added the lettering and colour, then corrected the parts that did not read clearly. I repeated that work sixteen times.
>
> The finished book gave those service stories a form that could be read and passed around. It also showed me that I could take one commission from the first conversation through the final layout.

## Completion decision

The audit found **25 visible or structural candidates**. It recommends retaining **8 factual or operational items**, rewriting **10 items**, and removing **7 redundant, abstract, or slogan-like items**. The proposed direction preserves the project facts, artwork, role credit, operating instructions, and accessible content. It removes named writing devices, layout narration, value slogans, repeated titles, and unearned reflective language.
