# The SEWA Chronicles: Writing-Style Research

## Research finding

The present book copy is weakest where it starts with a broad claim and then explains it. For this project, the writing should begin with a witnessed work moment, establish a specific constraint, and let the creative decision become clear through action.

Three public references informed the recommendation:

| Source | Applicable technique | What to avoid copying |
|---|---|---|
| [Indeed Design: *The Art of Storytelling for Case Studies*](https://indeed.design/article/the-art-of-storytelling-for-case-studies/) | A project story requires a background, a process, and an outcome. The middle should show why individual work steps mattered, not merely display deliverables. | UX case-study labels, achievement language, and heroic plot framing. |
| [Tobias van Schneider: *How to write project case studies for your portfolio*](https://vanschneider.com/blog/portfolio-tips/write-project-case-studies-portfolio/) | A reader needs a brief account of context, role, choices, and outcome. Captions should make visual material comprehensible when skimmed. | Portfolio self-promotion, over-explanation, and generic statements about craft. |
| [Creative Nonfiction: *Picturing the Personal Essay*](https://creativenonfiction.org/writing/picturing-the-personal-essay-a-visual-guide/) | Personal work benefits from a concrete narrative lift: a question or pressure that advances through scenes. Strong endings return to an opening image while leaving a credible future-facing implication. | Abstract reflection disconnected from lived detail, or a chronological list of events. |

## Constraints for SEWA copy

The book should not claim that the project was transformative, meaningful, or “a journey” without evidence. It should name a place, a task, an observed person, a decision, a material constraint, or a page being made. It should not make the reader admire ambition in the abstract. Instead, it should let the scale of the work become evident through the situation: a housekeeper, early in a career, accepting responsibility for a sixteen-page illustrated publication.

## Narrative shapes reviewed before approval

The following styles were reviewed before any copy changes:

1. **The Floor Notebook** — Scene-led, plain first person. Each spread begins with an object or moment from the resort floor and moves only as far as the evidence permits.
2. **The Working Proof** — A concise editorial ledger. Each page follows one question, one decision, and one artefact, with minimal introspection.
3. **The Letter from the Making** — An intimate but controlled retrospective letter to the earlier self who accepted the commission, returning at the close to the same decision.
4. **The Comic Editor’s Notes** — Short annotations that sit beside the original comic pages, explaining why a particular moment made the cut and what it needed from the drawing.

## Decision after approval

The user rejected named writing devices and asked for a normal human-written project post. The published book therefore uses no named format. It states the commission, the role, the reporting, the production process, the original comic pages, the finished result, and the credit in direct first-person language. The approaches above remain only as a research record; they do not supply labels, framing, or copy in the reader.
