# Visual Learning Expansion Research

## Source-backed course design decisions

Articulate’s current Rise guide frames a course as sections containing lessons, with lessons built one block at a time from text, media, interactions, and quizzes. It also distinguishes focused microlearning from a longer course and explicitly advises a learner-preview quality check before sharing.[1] Entertrainer will therefore keep its current course progression, but expand each existing lesson with a consistent teaching rhythm: a concrete hook, brief explanation, real visual evidence, an original explanatory visual when needed, a learner action, rationale-based feedback, and a short transfer recap.

The Dartmouth account provides an appropriate factual anchor for the AI-history expansion: it describes the 1956 Summer Research Project as a seminal field-forming event and quotes John McCarthy’s proposal about describing aspects of learning or intelligence precisely enough for a machine to simulate them.[2] The AI course will use this distinction carefully: the course will present Dartmouth as a landmark that named and organized a research field, rather than falsely suggesting that AI appeared suddenly in 1956.

NASA’s Intelligent Systems Division provides an applied counterweight to the chatbot-only view of AI. Its work spans autonomous systems and robotics, collaborative and assistant systems, systems health, and robust software engineering; the page describes free-flying Astrobee robots, autonomy projects, engineering software, and data systems used for mission work.[3] The expanded AI course will use real, attributed NASA material as evidence that modern AI operates in physical systems, scientific work, and human-supervised mission contexts.

UW Bothell’s ADDIE description identifies Analyze, Design, Develop, Implement, and Evaluate as a thinking device rather than a rigid linear sequence. Its analysis phase emphasizes the problem, learner goals, environment, existing knowledge, skills, and resources; its design phase brings objectives, assessment, exercises, media, and sequencing together; its development phase covers storyboards, writing, graphics, practice, and feedback.[4] The instructional-design course will use this as the basis for a more tangible studio workflow and original visual artefacts.

## Expanded source and asset policy

Only public-domain, compatible Creative Commons, clearly reusable government media, user-permitted stock media, or purpose-built original illustrations will be placed directly in the courses. Every direct-use external visual will receive an entry in `docs/asset_licenses.md` with source URL, creator where stated, license, and specific lesson use. Institutional pages, commercial course examples, and branded screenshots will be cited or linked but not copied as images without clear permission.

| Course area | Expanded learning treatment | Candidate primary sources and asset direction |
|---|---|---|
| **AI history** | Primary-source hook, historical photo, timeline evidence card, sequencing game | Dartmouth’s 1955/56 proposal history; public-domain Wikimedia images of Alan Turing and ENIAC; original annotations and interactions. |
| **AI systems today** | Real autonomy/robotics case, visual system stack, observation game, infrastructure reality check | NASA Intelligent Systems Division and Astrobee media subject to NASA media guidance; CC BY-SA Wikimedia CNN diagram with required attribution; original explanatory overlays. |
| **Prediction and language** | Context-to-token visual, story continuation microgame, fact hook, review card | Existing next-token lab, strengthened with original editorial sequences and sourced conceptual evidence rather than copying commercial model artwork. |
| **Instructional analysis** | Workplace evidence gallery, cause-versus-training mini challenge, task-step sorting | Existing real workplace imagery, verified public or licensed workplace artefacts, and original task-analysis diagrams. |
| **Design and development** | Storyboard repair lab, objective–practice–evidence animation, ADDIE studio stations | UW Bothell ADDIE guidance; CC-licensed Bloom’s taxonomy visual with credit; original storyboard, flow, and feedback visuals. |
| **Motion and games** | Short unlock reveals, progress changes, answer feedback, optional replay | CSS/Vue transitions using opacity and transform, native buttons, visible status text, and a complete `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback. |
